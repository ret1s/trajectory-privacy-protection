Continuous location queries expose an ordered transcript whose disclosure cannot be assessed
from isolated noisy coordinates alone. We study GeoI-BQ, a road-aware query mechanism combining
a geo-indistinguishable protected reference with a separately bounded publication channel.
A private reuse test accounts for decisions to retain or refresh the reference. Protected-history
beliefs adjust a centered distribution over jointly selected query segments, while a public
summable allowance schedule bounds disclosure without fixing the trip duration. Under explicit
support, timing, state and provider assumptions, we derive an end-to-end transcript bound,
prior-dependent localization bounds for arbitrary estimators, and conditional multi-channel
composition. Utility results distinguish planning-score gain, exact local ranking, bounded
deviation from a matched public base, and conditional transfer to service quality.
A controlled comparison evaluates six implementations on a previously inspected synthetic
cohort, with 20 test families and 120 retained trip-draws. GeoI-BQ attains 99.595\% session
Recall@5, with a 95\% interval of [99.224, 99.903]\%, and 0--0.188\% localization Hit100
across the five principal tasks in the selected attack bank. It uses 155 coordinate queries
per 600-second session. These observations do not establish faithful reproduction of the
compared papers, unrestricted anonymity, or utility superiority: the nonadaptive public base
performs slightly better. A data-access audit also shows near-complete static-catalogue
coverage and an untested post-budget regime. The resulting contribution is a conditional,
auditable transcript mechanism and evaluation, rather than a claim of deployment certification.
